# Learn from native monitors before narrowing

CodexBar and Tinybar suggest that a focused monitor would gain more from **clearer information boundaries and less background machinery than from becoming “native”**: both already use native macOS interfaces. Their source reveals useful protocol knowledge, credential-ownership safeguards and work-avoidance patterns, but neither establishes a quiet, maintenance-free baseline. The user's reports of excessive features, updates, breakage, lag and confusing information motivate discovery; they do not identify a cause. **Initial scope is Codex and Claude Code only**, without assumptions about accounts, plans, interest in quota versus money, or preferred display. This report separates established implementation behavior from hypotheses and leaves product choices open. The workspace began empty; no application implementation exists or has started.

## Native implementation does not prevent scope growth

The evidence notes inspect CodexBar at **`897bff31731bb4b21b105f6e8bf4c32a8a8bf095`**, a development commit starting 0.83.1, and Tinybar at **`79cb32f7e71eacb9e8ce45508dd00fac26a08d53`**, release 0.8.0, on November 1, 2026. CodexBar's latest published release at inspection was 0.83.0, not the inspected development version. This synthesis reads both notes and checks version-specific public release metadata; the underlying investigation used source, fixtures and public GitHub metadata. No builds, tests, benchmarks, app launches or authenticated requests were performed, and no installed applications, credentials, private transcripts or browser profiles were accessed. Implemented private protocols are not independently validated public contracts. ([CodexBar revision](https://github.com/steipete/CodexBar/commit/897bff31731bb4b21b105f6e8bf4c32a8a8bf095), [Tinybar revision](https://github.com/ThiloReintjes/tinybar/commit/79cb32f7e71eacb9e8ce45508dd00fac26a08d53))

CodexBar combines SwiftUI with AppKit menus; its breadth includes separate core, CLI, widget and helper targets, browser integration and plugin support. Tinybar uses an AppKit status item and popover hosting SwiftUI, with no third-party Swift packages declared. Yet Tinybar already includes five providers, persistent history, analytics, forecasts, incident feeds, notifications and an updater. Other providers matter here only as **evidence of expanding scope**, not candidates for this user's initial monitor. Neither a native framework nor a small dependency manifest determines product simplicity. ([CodexBar targets](https://github.com/steipete/CodexBar/blob/897bff31731bb4b21b105f6e8bf4c32a8a8bf095/Package.swift#L182-L225), [Tinybar interface](https://github.com/ThiloReintjes/tinybar/blob/79cb32f7e71eacb9e8ce45508dd00fac26a08d53/Sources/Tinybar/StatusItemController.swift#L1-L54), [Tinybar scope](https://github.com/ThiloReintjes/tinybar/blob/79cb32f7e71eacb9e8ce45508dd00fac26a08d53/README.md#L47-L65))

## “Usage” describes four different kinds of information

**Quota windows** answer how much allowance is used or remaining and when it resets. Codex reports used percentage, reset timestamp and window duration; durations, not array position or plan-name assumptions, distinguish windows. Claude reports utilization and reset dates for five-hour, weekly and scoped/model limits. Neither is a universal token allowance. Tinybar displays remaining percentage, while its canonical model stores used percentage: the same number without its direction is ambiguous. ([Codex normalization](https://github.com/steipete/CodexBar/blob/897bff31731bb4b21b105f6e8bf4c32a8a8bf095/Sources/CodexBarCore/Providers/Codex/CodexRateWindowNormalizer.swift#L3-L52), [Claude mapping](https://github.com/steipete/CodexBar/blob/897bff31731bb4b21b105f6e8bf4c32a8a8bf095/Sources/CodexBarCore/Providers/Claude/ClaudeUsageFetcher.swift#L955-L1041), [remaining convention](https://github.com/ThiloReintjes/tinybar/blob/79cb32f7e71eacb9e8ce45508dd00fac26a08d53/Sources/TinybarCore/Models.swift#L58-L83))

**Credits and extra usage** are separate from quota percentages. Claude's extra-usage fields represent monetary amounts converted from cents. Codex exposes balances and cap metadata; reset-credit inventory represents grants to reset limits, not expenditure. Even “credits” therefore needs qualification. A missing balance is not necessarily zero, and failure of optional financial enrichment need not invalidate valid quota data. ([Codex balance handling](https://github.com/steipete/CodexBar/blob/897bff31731bb4b21b105f6e8bf4c32a8a8bf095/Sources/CodexBarCore/Providers/Codex/CodexProviderDescriptor.swift#L531-L615), [Tinybar independent requests](https://github.com/ThiloReintjes/tinybar/blob/79cb32f7e71eacb9e8ce45508dd00fac26a08d53/Sources/TinybarCore/Codex/CodexUsageAPI.swift#L114-L157))

**Local history** records supported logs on this machine, not complete account consumption. Claude transcripts require deduplication of repeated or copied responses; Codex rollouts require cumulative-count and replay handling. Compressed historical Codex logs introduce coverage gaps in both projects. Local calendar-day summaries are not quota-reset windows, and missing history does not establish zero activity. ([Claude deduplication](https://github.com/ThiloReintjes/tinybar/blob/79cb32f7e71eacb9e8ce45508dd00fac26a08d53/Sources/TinybarCore/Usage/UsageStore.swift#L537-L568), [Codex coverage](https://github.com/steipete/CodexBar/blob/897bff31731bb4b21b105f6e8bf4c32a8a8bf095/docs/codex.md#L374-L430))

**API-equivalent estimates** price observed tokens; they are not subscription charges or invoices. Tinybar uses current model prices, so historical dollar equivalents can change. Its plan-value extrapolation divides local estimated cost by observed account-wide weekly utilization, quantities whose coverage can differ across devices and accounts. That is another estimate, not evidence of this user's subscription value. These distinctions explain potential confusion without establishing which information the user wants. ([Pricing](https://github.com/ThiloReintjes/tinybar/blob/79cb32f7e71eacb9e8ce45508dd00fac26a08d53/Sources/TinybarCore/Pricing/Pricing.swift#L3-L100), [extrapolation](https://github.com/ThiloReintjes/tinybar/blob/79cb32f7e71eacb9e8ce45508dd00fac26a08d53/Sources/TinybarCore/Usage/PlanValue.swift#L145-L165))

## Source selection changes authority, permissions and recovery

For **Codex**, CodexBar Auto orders personal-access token → OAuth → CLI, skipping unavailable strategies. A selected managed workspace removes CLI from Auto because that path cannot carry the selected workspace header. OAuth normally calls `chatgpt.com/backend-api/wham/usage`; ordinary network/server/decode failures do not indiscriminately trigger CLI fallback. Explicit Web is separate, and optional browser dashboard extras run after provider collection rather than forming Auto's final quota fallback. Conditional monthly-credit enrichment can nevertheless invoke CLI after OAuth succeeds, so “OAuth source” does not guarantee no subprocess. ([Source planning](https://github.com/steipete/CodexBar/blob/897bff31731bb4b21b105f6e8bf4c32a8a8bf095/Sources/CodexBarCore/Providers/Codex/CodexProviderDescriptor.swift#L152-L174), [fallback classification](https://github.com/steipete/CodexBar/blob/897bff31731bb4b21b105f6e8bf4c32a8a8bf095/Sources/CodexBarCore/Providers/Codex/CodexProviderDescriptor.swift#L492-L528), [conditional enrichment](https://github.com/steipete/CodexBar/blob/897bff31731bb4b21b105f6e8bf4c32a8a8bf095/Sources/CodexBarCore/Providers/Codex/CodexProviderDescriptor.swift#L679-L712))

Tinybar instead tries OAuth credentials from `CODEX_HOME/auth.json`, then Codex CLI only for missing/expired login, then explicitly enabled browser fallback if CLI fails. It does not substitute browsers for ordinary network or rate-limit errors. Both projects use structured `codex … app-server` RPC, notably `account/rateLimits/read`, rather than scraping `/status`. Tinybar's browser route exchanges ChatGPT session cookies for an access token before requesting usage. CLI delegation avoids implementing token renewal directly, but the child can renew its own credentials and introduces version, timeout and process-lifecycle dependencies. ([Tinybar selection](https://github.com/ThiloReintjes/tinybar/blob/79cb32f7e71eacb9e8ce45508dd00fac26a08d53/Sources/TinybarCore/Codex/CodexProvider.swift#L19-L52), [RPC](https://github.com/ThiloReintjes/tinybar/blob/79cb32f7e71eacb9e8ce45508dd00fac26a08d53/Sources/TinybarCore/Codex/CodexAppServer.swift#L101-L205), [browser exchange](https://github.com/ThiloReintjes/tinybar/blob/79cb32f7e71eacb9e8ce45508dd00fac26a08d53/Sources/TinybarCore/Codex/ChatGPTWebSession.swift#L3-L33))

For **Claude**, CodexBar's ambient macOS **app** Auto plans OAuth → CLI → Web; its macOS **CLI** Auto plans Web → CLI. The main Linux CLI pipeline can include OAuth. Selected account credentials constrain authority, and ambient Admin credentials select a different, organizational data product. Shared planner code does not mean identical availability or permission inputs across entry points. OAuth calls Anthropic's `/api/oauth/usage` with a beta header and optional reset-inventory query. Claude CLI collection is substantially different from Codex RPC: it launches an interactive PTY, parses `/usage`, and obtains identity through `/status`, with startup, trust and cleanup safeguards. Background launches are permission-gated; successful CLI results can be reused for **15 minutes**, preserving their original observation time. ([Planner](https://github.com/steipete/CodexBar/blob/897bff31731bb4b21b105f6e8bf4c32a8a8bf095/Sources/CodexBarCore/Providers/Claude/ClaudeSourcePlanner.swift#L170-L209), [PTY commands](https://github.com/steipete/CodexBar/blob/897bff31731bb4b21b105f6e8bf4c32a8a8bf095/Sources/CodexBarCore/Providers/Claude/ClaudeStatusProbe.swift#L140-L175), [cache](https://github.com/steipete/CodexBar/blob/897bff31731bb4b21b105f6e8bf4c32a8a8bf095/Sources/CodexBarCore/Providers/Claude/ClaudeCLIUsageSpawnThrottle.swift#L3-L44))

CodexBar's Claude OAuth loader combines an explicit environment override, owned caches, profile credential files and consent/policy-gated Keychain repair; it is not simply “read the CLI token.” Tinybar reads Claude credential files before Keychain, rejects expired credentials, and has **no Claude CLI quota fallback**. Missing/expired login can trigger explicitly enabled browser fallback using a `claude.ai` session and organization usage endpoint. Unlike CodexBar's broader non-cancellation OAuth Auto fallback, Tinybar does not generally switch sources after arbitrary failures. Direct HTTP avoids PTY parsing but retains private endpoint, beta-header and credential-format dependencies; browser access adds cookie decryption, permissions and organization selection. ([CodexBar loader](https://github.com/steipete/CodexBar/blob/897bff31731bb4b21b105f6e8bf4c32a8a8bf095/Sources/CodexBarCore/Providers/Claude/ClaudeOAuth/ClaudeOAuthCredentials.swift#L336-L489), [Tinybar credentials](https://github.com/ThiloReintjes/tinybar/blob/79cb32f7e71eacb9e8ce45508dd00fac26a08d53/Sources/TinybarCore/Claude/ClaudeCredentials.swift#L34-L95), [provider](https://github.com/ThiloReintjes/tinybar/blob/79cb32f7e71eacb9e8ce45508dd00fac26a08d53/Sources/TinybarCore/Claude/ClaudeProvider.swift#L19-L31), [CodexBar fallback](https://github.com/steipete/CodexBar/blob/897bff31731bb4b21b105f6e8bf4c32a8a8bf095/Sources/CodexBarCore/Providers/Claude/ClaudeProviderDescriptor.swift#L778-L804))

**Credential reuse is shared ownership, not merely reading a token.** Redeeming another application's rotating refresh token risks contention with its owner. CodexBar's native Codex path deliberately avoids that redemption and writing native auth, using constrained owner-CLI recovery instead; Tinybar documents read-only credential reuse. Delegation still permits the owner child to write. Account correctness remains separate: Tinybar chooses the first matching browser/profile and an eligible Claude organization, while snapshots lack account identity. Successful fallback can therefore report a different account from local history. CodexBar's scope guards illustrate why some apparently extra machinery protects correctness. The user's account arrangement and provider approval of these private integrations remain unverified. ([Ownership boundary](https://github.com/steipete/CodexBar/blob/897bff31731bb4b21b105f6e8bf4c32a8a8bf095/Sources/CodexBarCore/Providers/Codex/CodexProviderDescriptor.swift#L394-L477), [read-only policy](https://github.com/ThiloReintjes/tinybar/blob/79cb32f7e71eacb9e8ce45508dd00fac26a08d53/docs/adr/0001-read-only-credentials.md#L3-L12), [browser selection](https://github.com/ThiloReintjes/tinybar/blob/79cb32f7e71eacb9e8ce45508dd00fac26a08d53/Sources/TinybarCore/Cookies/BrowserCookies.swift#L5-L88), [snapshot fields](https://github.com/ThiloReintjes/tinybar/blob/79cb32f7e71eacb9e8ce45508dd00fac26a08d53/Sources/TinybarCore/Models.swift#L158-L187))

## Freshness and responsiveness require different evidence

**Attempt time, successful observation time and upstream freshness are different.** Both apps define their principal stale flag through errors, not age alone. CodexBar suppresses some transient failures and retains previous data; its Codex weekly-reset confirmation can intentionally delay publication after successful collection. Tinybar retains failed-provider snapshots and explains their age, but its global “Checked” time follows history-task completion, not every provider's success. An old number can reflect caching, permissions or publication policy rather than a stopped timer. Local RPC receipt time also does not prove fresh upstream revalidation. ([CodexBar stale definition](https://github.com/steipete/CodexBar/blob/897bff31731bb4b21b105f6e8bf4c32a8a8bf095/Sources/CodexBar/UsageStore/UsageStore.swift#L706-L720), [reset confirmation](https://github.com/steipete/CodexBar/blob/897bff31731bb4b21b105f6e8bf4c32a8a8bf095/Sources/CodexBar/Providers/Codex/CodexWeeklyResetConfirmation.swift#L81-L149), [Tinybar refresh completion](https://github.com/ThiloReintjes/tinybar/blob/79cb32f7e71eacb9e8ce45508dd00fac26a08d53/Sources/Tinybar/AppModel.swift#L191-L198))

Useful reusable patterns include coalesced refreshes, bounded child processes, Retry-After handling, independent optional enrichment and rejection of outdated account/configuration results. CodexBar moves provider collection off MainActor; opening its root menu does not synchronously fetch quota. Tinybar polls every five minutes, pauses new work during sleep/lock, requests refresh on wake/unlock and redraws its status item only when its display key changes. Already-running work is not cancelled merely by pause. These are concrete work-avoidance mechanisms, not comparative performance results. ([CodexBar execution boundary](https://github.com/steipete/CodexBar/blob/897bff31731bb4b21b105f6e8bf4c32a8a8bf095/Sources/CodexBar/UsageStore/UsageStore+Refresh.swift#L350-L390), [Tinybar lifecycle](https://github.com/ThiloReintjes/tinybar/blob/79cb32f7e71eacb9e8ce45508dd00fac26a08d53/Sources/Tinybar/AppModel.swift#L92-L198), [redraw guard](https://github.com/ThiloReintjes/tinybar/blob/79cb32f7e71eacb9e8ce45508dd00fac26a08d53/Sources/Tinybar/StatusItemController.swift#L65-L96))

Potential stalls remain hypotheses: CodexBar performs menu/layout and some projection work on MainActor; Tinybar synchronously initializes storage and checks credentials during MainActor startup. Browser hydration, CLI startup and history processing introduce other work. **No latency, CPU, memory or energy comparison was measured**, and Tinybar's README figures are not independent evidence. Likewise, permissive decoding becoming “No limits reported” is an inferred schema-drift risk, not a reproduced outage. Tests inspected upstream offer useful parsing/security/lifecycle cases, not certification of either app or diagnosis of the installed CodexBar. ([CodexBar projection](https://github.com/steipete/CodexBar/blob/897bff31731bb4b21b105f6e8bf4c32a8a8bf095/Sources/CodexBar/UsageStore/UsageStore+TokenCost.swift#L310-L317), [Tinybar initialization](https://github.com/ThiloReintjes/tinybar/blob/79cb32f7e71eacb9e8ce45508dd00fac26a08d53/Sources/Tinybar/AppModel.swift#L16-L75), [decoder](https://github.com/ThiloReintjes/tinybar/blob/79cb32f7e71eacb9e8ce45508dd00fac26a08d53/Sources/TinybarCore/Claude/ClaudeUsageAPI.swift#L30-L38))

## Quiet maintenance requires more than fewer providers

CodexBar published four releases from October 23–31; Tinybar moved from 0.6.0 on October 23 to 0.8.0 on November 1. These short historical samples do not predict future cadence or establish quieter updates in Tinybar. CodexBar's pinned changelog mixes compatibility repairs with feature work; Tinybar 0.8.0 adds another provider. Narrowing to Codex and Claude Code removes unrelated surfaces, but cannot freeze their authentication, endpoint schemas or CLI behavior. ([CodexBar release history](https://github.com/steipete/CodexBar/blob/897bff31731bb4b21b105f6e8bf4c32a8a8bf095/CHANGELOG.md#L5-L145), [Tinybar 0.6.0 release record](https://api.github.com/repos/ThiloReintjes/tinybar/releases/422178112), [Tinybar 0.8.0 release record](https://api.github.com/repos/ThiloReintjes/tinybar/releases/430853754))

Release frequency, checking, prompting and installation are separate burdens. CodexBar routes by installation origin: ordinary signed app installs use Sparkle with separate check/install choices, both initially enabled for new ordinary app installs; Homebrew checks daily without automatic installation. Tinybar checks daily, retries failures hourly and requires user action; replacing Sparkle introduces a bespoke Homebrew updater rather than eliminating update complexity. Neither project's behavior establishes this user's current settings. ([CodexBar updater selection](https://github.com/steipete/CodexBar/blob/897bff31731bb4b21b105f6e8bf4c32a8a8bf095/Sources/CodexBar/CodexbarApp.swift#L151-L232), [Homebrew scheduling](https://github.com/steipete/CodexBar/blob/897bff31731bb4b21b105f6e8bf4c32a8a8bf095/Sources/CodexBar/HomebrewUpdater.swift#L195-L229), [Tinybar checking](https://github.com/ThiloReintjes/tinybar/blob/79cb32f7e71eacb9e8ce45508dd00fac26a08d53/Sources/TinybarCore/Updates/ReleaseCheck.swift#L14-L92), [Tinybar installer](https://github.com/ThiloReintjes/tinybar/blob/79cb32f7e71eacb9e8ce45508dd00fac26a08d53/Sources/Tinybar/Updater.swift#L97-L182))

Both root projects are **MIT-licensed**; copying requires retaining applicable notices, with dependency/vendored obligations checked separately. Protocol findings and focused fixtures are reusable without adopting either entire framework. Features to leave uncommitted unless needed include browser access, local history/pricing, plan-value forecasts, multi-account switching, plugins, sync, widgets, alerts and incident feeds. Account identity, missing-data semantics and honest freshness are different: they protect meaning rather than expand the product. ([CodexBar license](https://github.com/steipete/CodexBar/blob/897bff31731bb4b21b105f6e8bf4c32a8a8bf095/LICENSE#L1-L21), [Tinybar license](https://github.com/ThiloReintjes/tinybar/blob/79cb32f7e71eacb9e8ce45508dd00fac26a08d53/LICENSE#L1-L21))

## Conclusion

The next conversation should begin with experience, not architecture: **which concrete CodexBar interaction or number is confusing, laggy or broken, and what decision was the user trying to make?** Then ask which account/organization arrangements and clients are involved; which of quota/reset, credits/extra usage, local history or estimates actually matters; and what freshness, credential-access and update interruptions are acceptable. Display preferences can follow those examples rather than being inferred from either upstream interface.

The important discovery is that reducing features and reducing ambiguity are related but distinct. A smaller monitor can still present the wrong account, an old observation or an estimate as fact. Conversely, some upstream complexity exists precisely to prevent those mistakes. This report supports an unhurried discussion of which responsibilities are necessary, not a quota-first commitment, source selection, UI concept or settled specification.
